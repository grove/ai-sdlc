# R08 Verify that the verified artifact is the one being delivered

<a id="r08"></a>

- **Stage:** [Production](../stages/production.md)
- **Category:** [Preparation and release decision](../stages/production.md#r-category-1)
- **Operating Mode:** Advisory
- **Especially Relevant To:** Platform Engineering, Regulated Systems, Embedded and OT

## Opportunity

AI can explain and collate provenance, signature control and artifact identities along the supply chain. It marks when a building has been changed or cannot be linked to a test certificate. This supports control of the transition from verified result to distributed package without the model itself acting as an integrity check.

## Inputs and Evidence

Artifact hashes, build identities, signature verification, software inventory, test evidence and log from approved artifact registries and deployment processes.

## Prerequisites

Buildings and artifacts must have stable identities, and proof of integrity must come from controlled tools without model-generated attestation.

## Example and Deliverable

Before publishing a library, AI creates a compliance report that links the package hash to the tested commit and shows that one platform variant lacks a build proof.

## Safeguards and Review

Cryptographic and deterministic mechanisms control integrity. The release owner assesses deviations, and unknown or changed artefacts must be verified before further delivery.

## Measuring Impact

Time to clarify artifact traceability, together with deliveries without valid provenance and discrepancies between tested and distributed content.

## Applying This Idea

Clarify what intended use means for the delivery, who approves the transfer and who owns the operation. Follow the introduction with measurements and a workable plan for failure.
